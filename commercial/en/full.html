01 / PRICING LOGIC

The pricing logic in one line

Why evidence, not inference, is the billable unit
We do not charge for judgement; we sell the evidence judgement leaves behind: a verifiable PCF label, a batch quality bond, and an auditable ledger. The grower buys judgement, the buyer buys evidence; one batch is paid for twice.
reason 01

Judgement's marginal cost tends to zero; evidence's does not

The judger is one forward pass: a fixed model, compute by the second, copyable in the cloud. Per-call pricing collapses to the server's marginal cost under competition, and pricing power ends up somewhere else.

The evidence chain does not collapse: metering happens in the physical world (sensors, energy meters), verification needs a third party, and liability needs a signature. Those costs do not fall when models get cheaper.

reason 02

Only evidence can be passed to the buyer

The grower's budget line is a cost item — every yuan has to come out of money not lost; the buyer's budget line is compliance and premium — the label is a condition of entry and scope 3 primary data.

Charging for judgement sells once (the grower); charging for evidence sells twice (the grower pays for judgement access, the buyer pays for the label and the bond). One batch, two payers, two budget logics.

reason 03

Evidence can be graded, therefore priced

Judgement has no grade, only "right or wrong" — it cannot go on a quote. Evidence has grades: a metered share of ≥70% / ≥45% / <45% maps directly to grades A / B / C and issuance fees of ¥80 / ¥60 / ¥40.

The grade is the only mechanical interface between quality and price, and it is what makes every unit price on this page hold up.

The most expensive commercial finding on this site hides in the subtraction between these three cards: self-build costs ¥34,000 a year to hold, against ¥28,000 of avoided loss At 60,000 plantlets a grower who buys the full hardware never pays it back — not a long payback, a divergent one. So the unit of pricing is not the farm but the Zone. One edge box at ¥18,000, amortised over 3 years and shared by 8 farms, costs ¥18,000 ÷ 3 ÷ 8 = ¥750/site/year derived. At 15 farms per zone the box drops to ¥400/site/year and a 60,000-plantlet site totals ¥3,100 a year derived. Diluted fixed cost is the only mechanical reason a small site's books can balance.

04 / EVIDENCE PRICING

Pricing the certificate: by evidence grade, not by batch

Per-batch, priced by evidence grade — the upsell engine

For the same 240-plantlet batch the label can cost ¥40, ¥60 or ¥80. The difference is not our labour, it is how much of the data is metered. The grade is what the buyer reads, the price is what the grower reads — and it is the same number.

Data grade Criterion (metered share) Label issuance fee Description
A ≥ 70% of line items metered ¥80 / batch the buyer's preferred grade; usable as scope 3 primary data
B ≥ 45% ¥60 / batch where AP-0712 sits today — the current deployment is at this grade
C < 45% ¥40 / batch needs more sensors to upgrade; the label still issues, but the buyer discounts it
The grade comes from one metered share — a single mechanical criterion, not a human rating. The grade freezes at issuance and enters the ledger with the label hash. 240,000 plantlets a year = 500 batches → grade A ¥40,000 / grade B ¥30,000 / grade C ¥20,000 derived.

05 / ANTI-ARBITRAGE

Anti-arbitrage rules (four, hard-coded)

Hard rules — non-negotiable, checked before issuance

The hole in per-batch pricing is not in the price list but in splitting and double issuance. These four rules are not decoration in the terms; they are checks that must pass before issuance — if one fails, the system emits no label.

# Hard rule Enforcement Why it is hard-coded
R1 240 plantlets minimum per batch below 240 no label issues; a hard system check with no manual exception metering noise and the fixed cost of issuance are out of proportion; below this size the label is noise
R2 same lineage within 24 hours bills as one batch; splitting is not allowed billing aggregates by lineage id over a 24 h sliding window; split sub-batches merge back automatically splitting is the only arbitrage that turns ¥40 a batch into N × ¥40
R3 one batch maps to one harvest window the harvest-window id binds a unique batch number; nothing merges or inherits across windows evidence maps to one physical event; a label merged across windows describes cargo that does not exist
R4 one batch cannot be issued twice; issuance freezes the hash unique index on the batch number; after issuance the hash is on the chain and frozen, and a revision takes a new version number instead of overwriting a label that can be edited is not evidence; selling one certificate twice zeroes the credibility of the whole ledger
Without these four, per-batch pricing sells not evidence but invoices.

One practical addition: the four rules must live in the issuance service's code, not only in the contract. Rules in a contract let arbitrage happen around it; rules in the validator reject it at the first API call. R1's 240-plantlet floor also explains every batch count here: 240,000 ÷ 480 = 500 batches, and 480 = 2 × 240 derived.

06 / GOVERNANCE

Who is speaking: the issuer must be separate from the seller

Neutrality — why the issuer cannot be the vendor

If the body that issues the label is the same body that sells hardware, SaaS and bonds, the label is not evidence but marketing collateral. That is not a moral point, it is the rating-agency conflict.

why split now

Why separate it now

payer = beneficiary is the rating-agency conflict: the one paying is also the one selling the label, and its independence is bought out on day one.

A more practical reason: separating 18 months from now costs ten times more. By then it is not an org chart that moves but historical labels, frozen hashes and sold bonds — every one of which must answer "who signed it".

data rights

Data rights: written into the contract template

Raw data belongs to the grower — raw IoT readings, judgement logs and batch details do not change owner by being connected.

Aggregated, de-identified benchmarks belong to the platform — cross-site, de-identified, non-attributable benchmarks used for grading and PCF baselines.

Both sentences belong in the contract template, not the privacy policy. A privacy policy can be amended unilaterally; a contract template cannot.

baseline co-sign

Baseline co-signing: never set unilaterally by the platform

The baseline is frozen by both parties at contract signature, not set by the platform. It is what makes a number like "53.5% reduction" challengeable — the only entry point: a baseline that can be co-signed can be questioned, and one that can be questioned can be relied on.

Arbitration: the frozen baseline snapshot is written into the ledger and either party may open a review from it; a unilateral change is technically impossible because the snapshot is hash-frozen.

current gap

The label can only print PENDING today

/carbon/ The page still marks not yet verified: the method is written and the example has been run, but the verifier has not been contracted.

Until that contract exists the label may only print PENDING — it may issue, enter the ledger and carry a price, but it may not claim verification. That rule does not loosen because sales would like it to.

Governance is the most expensive line on this page and the cheapest risk control. Splitting costs two sets of books, two audits, two legal teams; not splitting costs every label failing at once the first time a buyer asks. The first price is knowable, the second is not.
