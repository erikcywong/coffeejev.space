PUBLIC METHODOLOGY · COFFEE DECISION LAYER

Models are replaceable;
the standard is the product.

CoffeeJeV turns twelve-field IoT telemetry into three kinds of decision: contamination, acclimatisation readiness and nutrient-fog selection. This page fixes the twelve rules, the version hash and the challenge process behind those decisions, so that replacing a model never changes the auditable criteria.

01

THESIS / WHY

Why the standard is the asset

A model is an implementation detail; a standard is an allocation of responsibility. Only once that sentence is written as auditable rules does anything have the right to be issued.

A model can be copied. Mapping twelve fields onto three decision classes is not complicated mathematics: a threshold, a linear penalty, a softmax ranking. An experienced engineer could rewrite it in two hours with comparable results. Any claim that model performance is the moat has to accept that first.

The standard cannot be copied. It is made of incident records, contract clauses, version history and interfaces that can be challenged. Which threshold came from one expensive false negative, which floor is a client constraint rather than a statistical finding, why an old label may not be quietly recomputed — none of that lives in the weights.

So what ships is a numbered rule set. CJV-MTH-1.0, twelve rules, method hash 9b12abef. The model may change; a rule may only change through an explicit upgrade that states its blast radius. Anyone can copy a judger. Almost nobody can copy a rule chain that has been audited, challenged and cited in a contract.

COPYABLE

Copying a judger

Model definition, inference code, training data. Measured in hours or days.

NOT COPYABLE

Copying this standard

Twelve rules, incident records, contract baselines, version history, challenge archive. Measured in audit cycles.

The implementation can be copied. The audited chain of judgement cannot.

02

RULE CHAIN

The 12-rule chain

Every rule states an id, a criterion and a challenge point. The criterion must resolve to a formula or a threshold; the challenge point must name a disputable input or constant, not an attitude.

Criterion = executable formula / threshold Challenge point = an assumption a counter-example can overturn
MTH-01Decision threshold

Cost-asymmetry threshold

Cost-asymmetric threshold

The relative cost of the two error types sets the interception probability; no contamination rate is assumed in advance.

Criterion
τ_cost = C_FP / (C_FP + C_FN) p ≥ τ_cost → hold side; p < τ_cost → release side

C_FP = expected cost of one false release; C_FN = expected cost of one false hold; p = posterior probability of contamination.

Challenge point Who prices the two costs, and how those prices are audited; where the costs come from the client contract, the threshold must be recomputed with the contract annex and entered in the version record.

MTH-02Operating constraint

lower operating limit

Operational floor

The operating floor may only raise the interception probability; no model may lower it on its own.

Criterion
τ_hi = max(τ_cost, τ_min) τ_min = 0.15 // default

Any τ_min below the default must be listed in the contract annex and written into the decision record's method_version and the label metadata.

Challenge point The floor is a business constraint, not a statistical finding. A client may replace it, but it must be written into the contract; otherwise it may not be used for issuance.

MTH-03Evidence bands

Two-threshold triage

Two-threshold evidence

Above the high threshold, isolate immediately; above the low threshold, require microscopy review, so an uncertain sample is never released outright.

Criterion
τ_lo = max(0.06, 0.45 · τ_hi)
p bandactionRecord required
p < τ_loReleaseStandard decision record
τ_lo ≤ p < τ_hiMicroscopy reviewReview conclusion written back; the case cannot be closed without it
p ≥ τ_hiQuarantineQuarantine batch id + disposal evidence

Challenge point 0.06 and 0.45 are engineering constants, not estimates from data; changing the crop or the cost of review requires re-validation.

MTH-04Hard gates

Hard-gate score cap

Hard-gate score cap

The weighted score only ranks above the gates; it cannot override a hard constraint.

Criterion
cap = 100 if N_root < 3: cap = min(cap, 55) if browning > 60%: cap = min(cap, 60) if p ≥ 0.5: cap = min(cap, 40) S_final = min(S_weighted, cap)

Challenge point The three caps come from incident reviews. When several gates fire at once only the lowest cap applies; whether that is enough to cover cross-cutting risk has to be tested with counter-examples.

MTH-05Output semantics

Abstention semantics

Abstention semantics

Abstention is a formal output, not an error code. It must be written to the ledger and counted in the statistics, never padded with a default action.

MTH-06Feasible set

Feasibility before utility

Feasibility before utility

Pass the hard constraints first, then compute utility, then rank only inside the feasible set. A high return may never compensate for a prohibited action.

Criterion
F = { a ∈ A | g_k(a) ≤ 0, ∀k = 1..K } if F = ∅ → no_safe_option + stop_and_review P(a) = exp(β · U(a)) / Σ exp(β · U(a')), a, a' ∈ F β = 6 a* = argmax P(a), a ∈ F

g_k is the hard-constraint function; U is the expected utility; β=6 is ranking sharpness and applies only inside the feasible set.

Challenge point Whether the hard-constraint set K is complete, and whether β=6 needs recalibration when the cost scale changes.

MTH-07Evidence freezing

Hash-chain freezing

Hash-chain freeze

Decision and label share one structure: any change to a field must change the hash, and an old record can never be overwritten.

Criterion
h_i = fnv(prev_hash ‖ canonical(payload_i)) canonical: keys in lexicographic order · UTF-8 · no redundant whitespace · decimal numerals

The payload skeleton holds method_version、inputs_digest、output、decided_at; the label uses the same skeleton and can be recomputed independently.

Challenge point FNV-1a-64 provides tamper detection only; it is not a collision-resistance commitment. External anchoring is the client's decision. A change to the canonicalisation implementation must ship as MAJOR and start a new chain.

Challenge point Whether the denominator includes estimated energy, whether the metering window is cumulative or continuous, and how lost samples are counted — each choice changes the grade, so all three must be written into the rule snapshot.

MTH-10Batch boundary

Batch integrity

Batch integrity

The batch is the smallest unit of issuance, not a billable unit that can be split.

Criterion
valid_batch ⇔ |B| ≥ 240 ∧ (max t_harvest − min t_harvest) ≤ 24h ∧ harvest_windows(B) = 1

Same lineage within 24 h merges into one batch; one batch maps to one harvest window; splitting to farm issuance fees is prohibited.

Challenge point The 240-plantlet floor is an operating constraint, not a statistical optimum; the 24 h boundary must be a closed interval; and where the client does not share lineage the rule cannot be executed.

MTH-11Reduction baseline

Baseline co-signing

Baseline co-sign

The reduction baseline is frozen by both parties at contract signature; the platform may neither set it unilaterally nor adjust it retroactively.

Criterion
B0 = (yield_ref, energy_ref, EF_ref, boundary, period) reduction = (E_ref − E_actual) / E_ref valid_reduction ⇔ sig_client(B0) ∧ sig_platform(B0)

Without both signatures no reduction statement may be issued; a baseline may only be updated through a contract annex, never swapped silently at runtime.

Challenge point Whether the co-signing party can actually verify the baseline, which reference year applies when history is missing, and whether baseline updates may only travel through the contract annex.

MTH-12Version governance

Versions cannot be edited retroactively

Non-retroactive versioning

A method change must ship a new version number; old labels point at the old version forever, and silent recomputation or overwrite is prohibited.

Criterion
version = CJV-MTH-{MAJOR}.{MINOR}[.{PATCH}] MAJOR = immutable item or semantic change MINOR = threshold / parameter change PATCH = editorial fix that changes no criterion

Old labels' method_version cannot be modified; a new version produces new labels and may never write back to an old record.

Challenge point What counts as an editorial fix that changes no criterion needs a whitelist; otherwise PATCH becomes a way around the version record.

AUDIT RULE

Recompute by version, never by current version

Both the decision record and the carbon label carry method_version with method_hash. An audit takes the version from the record, loads the matching rule snapshot, then replays the input.

  1. 01 Read method_version and inputs_digest.
  2. 02 Load that version's immutable rule snapshot and threshold configuration.
  3. 03 Replay the input; recompute the decision / label and the ledger hash.
  4. 04 On mismatch, freeze the related issuance and open an incident; the old record must not be edited.

04

CHALLENGE

How to challenge a rule

A challenge is not a customer-service process. It requires reproducible evidence, a stated failure scenario and a computable blast radius. Thirty-day submission window, adjudication within fourteen days of acceptance, and the outcome is published.

01

Submit a challenge

D0–D30 / WINDOW

All material arrives in one submission inside the 30-day window; a submission outside it joins the next version discussion and does not block current issuance.

02

Completeness check

COMPLETENESS CHECK

Three items are checked; complete means docketed. One missing and it is returned once, without entering adjudication.

03

Adjudicate

≤ 14 DAYS

Only three outcomes are possible: uphold, revise, new version. The reasoning and a summary of the counter-example must be published.

04

Public archive

D44+ / PUBLIC RECORD

Archive the rule id, the version diff, the blast radius and the list of labels to recompute; old labels keep their original version.

REQUIRED 01

Counter-example data

The twelve-field raw record, timestamps and batch number; the smallest reproducible sample.

REQUIRED 02

Failure scenario

Under which input distribution the rule takes the wrong action, and what that error costs.

REQUIRED 03

Blast radius

The affected label_id / decision_id list, plus the range of labels and batches to recompute.

Outcomes

CHANGE CONTROL

What may change, what may not

Mutable items travel by version; immutable items travel by a new chain. Neither is something operations can quietly adjust.

Thresholdsτ_min, the τ_lo coefficient, gate constants; a change applies to new decisions only.

CLAIM BOUNDARY

What we refuse to claim

These six statements are product boundaries, not legal boilerplate. Sales material may not delete, reword or soften them.

REFUSE 01

Not a carbon credit

What we issue is a carbon footprint label and a decision record built on metered energy. It is not a tradable credit and carries no additionality argument.

REFUSE 02

Not an offset

There is no baseline additionality, no leakage provision and no permanence arrangement, so it may not be used to offset emissions in any scope.

REFUSE 03

Not certified

We cite ISO 14067, PAS 2050, the GHG Protocol, ISO/IEC 17025 and ISO 19011, but no accreditation body has certified us. A certification conclusion can only be issued by an accreditation body.

REFUSE 04

No yield prediction

This layer outputs contamination judgement, acclimatisation readiness and nutrient-fog selection only. Yield belongs to the client's own agronomy.

REFUSE 05

No pesticide prescription

It outputs release, review or isolation only. It never names a pesticide, a dose or an application, and plant-protection decisions remain with qualified personnel.

REFUSE 06

No plant-variety rights ruling

Lineage fields exist for batch merging and traceability only, never for authenticity, ownership or infringement judgements.
