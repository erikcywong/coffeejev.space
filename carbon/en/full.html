01 / WHY NOW

A metered number can be audited; a number in a spreadsheet is a guess

metered primary data vs. spreadsheet estimate — the only durable difference

EU product-footprint rules and the requirements of major buyers converge on one engineering constraint: prefer site-specific primary data and disclose the share of secondary data. Neither ISO 14067 nor PAS 2050 forbids estimation, but both require estimates and measurements to be booked and disclosed separately. Most agricultural labels fail deep verification not because the arithmetic is wrong but because both the denominator (yield) and the numerator (energy) come from a spreadsheet assembled after the fact, with no timestamps and no way to prove it was not edited. That is a structural cost problem, not an attitude problem: without metering hardware, primary data cannot be obtained at all.

Our fleet happens to have metering-grade sources for the two heaviest line items. power-meter-PuEr-01 reported every 60 s power_kw / kwh_total / tariff — a real energy increment from a cumulative register, not power × duration; fog-ctrl-A3 reports ec / ph / do / water_temp with fog_duty — combined with dosing-pump records, nitrogen input moves from "allocated from the purchase order" to "accumulated per mixing batch". Together these two account for 65.5% primary-data share (0.1273 / 0.1943) in the worked batch on this page. The remaining 34.5% is upstream secondary data — packaging, outbound transport, hardware amortisation — normally taken from supplier factor tables. It is replaceable and not a structural defect.

Timestamps are the second layer. The time resolution of the energy data decides which factor applies: the same kilowatt-hour differs by 87% between the 00–06 band (0.31 kgCO2e/kWh) and 17–21 (0.58). A figure built on an annual average erases real abatement like "move lighting and fogging into the night trough" — it neither rewards the right behaviour nor identifies the wrong configuration. This page's engine applies six hourly marginal-factor bands band by band to every metering interval (§3), so a change of light spectrum, rotation or tariff strategy shows up directly in the label number.

The third layer is the hash chain. At close, each batch ledger takes the canonical JSON of interval list + metering summary + recipe record + yield, hashes it with SHA-256, and writes the previous batch's hash into this one (prev_hash), forming an append-only batch chain that cannot be rewritten. The verifier receives not a PDF but an independently recomputable input set: they can multiply 134.4 kWh by the factor table themselves, get 57.232, and compare it with ours. Auditable means someone else can recompute it. The rest of what is sold as a "carbon platform" swaps an unverifiable estimate for an unverifiable dashboard.

path a · spreadsheet carbon

A number in a spreadsheet not auditable

  • Numerator: monthly bill ÷ seedlings, average factor, time resolution = one month
  • Denominator: dispatch-note totals, batch attribution reconciled by hand
  • Traceability: no raw series; a changed cell leaves no trace
  • Verification cost: the verifier can only sample and interview, so the conclusion can only be limited assurance
  • result: primary-data share ≈ 0, so an EU buyer's due diligence will rarely accept the label
path b · metered carbon

A metered number recomputable

  • Numerator：kwh_total cumulative register differenced per interval, 60 s granularity
  • Denominator: the batch id binds plant count and closing time, generated at T+0
  • Traceability: raw interval series + sha256 digest + prev_hash chain
  • Verification cost: the verifier recomputes instead of interviewing; the work drops from auditing books to running a formula
  • result: 65.5% primary data, with the secondary share disclosed, which is what 14067 requires

So "now" refers not to a market window but to the data conditions having just become true: the same telemetry already feeds both closed-loop control and the carbon ledger, so the marginal cost is near zero. Building labels first and meters later would mean rebuilding the data path; in the order we took — control layer → telemetry → batch ledger → label and points — each layer reuses the one below. That is the one genuinely differentiated argument on this page; the rest (factors, labels, points) is engineering detail a peer can copy.

02 / BOUNDARY & FUNCTIONAL UNIT

Boundary and functional unit: what is included, and why each exclusion holds

cradle-to-gate · ISO 14067 / PAS 2050 aligned · GHG Protocol scopes

The system boundary is cradle-to-gate: from upstream input production and nursery operation through delivery of the seedling at the gate (gate to gate, including outbound transport). It excludes the grow-out stage on the farm, consumer use and final disposal. Scope 1 / 2 / 3 follow the GHG Protocol, and every exclusion carries an auditable reason rather than "not counted for now".

Algorithm and factors: the formula chain, a worked batch, the hourly curve, and uncertainty

formula chain → worked example (480 plantlets) → hourly MEF → uncertainty

Every number is recomputable: given the factor table and the metering series, any third party should arrive at the same 57.232, 3.888 and 96.00. That is the only reason this method can be verified at all.

3.1 The formula chain engine formula chain

# 1) grid emissions: weighted per interval, not by an annual average factor E_grid,b = Σ ( ΔkWht × MEFt ) , ΔkWht = kwh_totalt − kwh_totalt−1 MEFt = band( local_hour(t) ) → { 00–06: 0.31 | 06–09: 0.38 | 09–12: 0.44 | 12–17: 0.47 | 17–21: 0.58 | 21–24: 0.41 } # 2) every other item: activity data × factor E_N = m_N × 13.5 // kg N × kgCO2e/kg N E_pack = n_out × 0.012 // sets dispatched E_trans= n_out × 0.020 // ambient trunk haul; cold chain ×1.6 E_hw = A_hw × 8 dev / 4 yr / Q_year // dedicated assets amortised over annual output E_computed = E_grid + E_N + E_pack + E_trans + E_hw E_unit = E_computed / n_batch E_declared = ceil (E_unit, 0.01) // rounding up is the conservative direction # 3) avoided emissions → points A_unit = 0.42 − E_declared // baseline 0.42 (range 0.34–0.52) P_batch = A_unit × n_batch × mult(grade) // 1 point = 1 kgCO2e avoided mult = { A: 1.0, B: 0.9, C: 0.8 } // data-quality grade Value = P × ¥0.10 // redeemable against hardware/SaaS only

The rounding direction is deliberate: rounding up keeps the declared value from ever falling below the computed one, at the cost of a 0.0057 kgCO2e/plantlet (2.9%) conservative bias. Auditors accept conservative; optimistic is unacceptable.

sensitivity · shifting the load

What moving the load into 00–06 saves

The six bands exist not to be more precise but to make load-shifting priceable. If lighting and fogging all move into 00–06 (0.31): 0.28 × 0.31 = 0.0868, electricity emissions fall from 0.1192 to 0.0868 (−27.2%), and the per-plantlet total becomes 0.1943 → 0.1618, declared value 0.17, reduction against the baseline 59.5%.

The reverse: if load concentrates in 17–21 (0.58), electricity per plantlet is 0.1624, total 0.2375, reduction falls to 43.5%. Same hardware, same output, a 17-point gap in the label number — which is why hourly factors are used rather than an annual mean.

Combination: u = √(0.0179² + 0.0016² + 0.0030² + 0.0060² + 0.0140²) = √0.0005636 = 0.02374; relative to the declared value 0.0237 / 0.200 = ±11.9%. The independence assumption is not strict (electricity and nitrogen both follow the recipe), so the figure is optimistic and in practice should be taken as ±15% .

the dominant term · this is where the uncertainty lives

Baseline uncertainty is four times the product's

Baseline 0.42 (range 0.34–0.52, ±0.09) is self-declared. Combined into the avoided quantity: √(0.0237² + 0.09²) = 0.0931, i.e. A = 0.22 ± 0.093 kgCO2e/plantlet (±42%). Carried into the reduction rate: 30%–75%; looking only at the baseline range with the actual fixed at 0.20 gives 41%–62%.

So in the "52%" the fragile part is not our 0.200 but the denominator, 0.42. Every public statement must read "relative to the conventional soil-nursery baseline this method defines", never "half of conventional".

precision vs accuracy

We spent precision where it does not matter

Metering to 60 s, six factor bands, declaring to 0.001 — these raise precision (recomputable, comparable, auditable). The headline result is limited by a baseline assumption and cannot raise accuracy (distance from the truth).

That is an acceptable methodological trade-off, but the public material must say it plainly: a precise ledger plus a rough baseline is a precise relative comparison, not an absolute truth. Internally the baseline range is maintained and versioned separately, and signed apart from the label hash.

4.4 Verification: from id and hash to a recomputation public verification

verifier path · anyone

Five steps, no account with us

  1. Scan or type the id → fetch the label record. The QR contains only label_id plus the first 12 hash digits, and no personal data.
  2. Recompute the hash → recompute SHA-256 over the record under the published canon and compare byte for byte with hash . A mismatch means the page or the record was edited.
  3. Recompute the emissions → multiply the record's activity × factors：134.4 × 0.425833 = 57.232, add line by line to 93.280, and check that ceil → 0.200. Any mismatch turns the page red.
  4. Check the chain → use prev_hash to walk back through batches to the genesis of the chain (offline; no bulk data needed). A broken link invalidates the label.
  5. Verify the raw data → request the raw interval series for inputs_digest and check it with the same digest algorithm. This step requires authorisation from the data holder (verifier, buyer due diligence, regulator); it is not fully public.
design rules

Four hard constraints

  • A hash cannot be written back: after closing, the record is read-only; a revision ships a new version (version+1) and keeps the old one
  • Digest granularity ≤ 60 s: raw intervals are kept for 24 months, then only the digest (the balance between storage and verifiability)
  • Factors are versioned：factors fully embedded in the record, never an external link — or it cannot be recomputed five years from now
  • No dependence on our domain: everything needed is in the record and the hash; the page is an entrance, not a precondition

Verification that requires logging into our platform is not verification; it is a display.

05 / POINTS ECONOMY

Points economics: the three-party ledger, and the arithmetic for 240,000 plantlets a year

grower · platform · buyer — 1 point = 1 kgCO2e avoided = ¥0.10, redeemable only in-kind

State the basis first: points are an internal reward voucher, redeemable only against our hardware and SaaS, not transferable, not redeemable for cash, not a carbon credit and not an offset. Their job is to lower the adoption barrier and lengthen retention, not to hand the grower a carbon income — the arithmetic below shows why.

5.1 The three-party ledger three-way ledger

honest reading · 1

Points are a discount, not income

The ¥4,752 a year of face value is only 6.1% of first-year hardware spend. Selling points as a benefit misleads the grower: it is a voucher to buy again, and the cost lands back on us (¥3,384). The real proposition is the premium and the compliance passport.

honest reading · 2

Steady-state margin cannot carry a company

Steady-state margin is ¥9,816 a year per site. Covering sales, R&D and the verification function requires adding sites continuously or raising the SaaS unit price (from ¥0.03 to ¥0.10 per plantlet a year is ¥24,000 a year). That is the model's real gap, not something to blur.

honest reading · 3

The platform's real money is verification and data

It retains ¥6,000 of the verification fee (40%), and that is renewed annually; third-party cost amortises with scale. The data asset is worth more as better factors and process advice in the next version — which is what lifts SaaS pricing power — than as something sold.

5.4 The premium channel's basis problem premium caveat

The premium is priced per kilogram of green beans (¥0.3–1.0/kg) while our functional unit is a seedling. Between the two there is a missing grow-out output coefficient (kg of green beans per plant per year). That coefficient has no metered baseline on this page, so 30 t is a scenario parameter only and must not be read as the output of 240,000 plantlets. The right sequence is to wait for the grow-out stage (FU 2) to get a metered baseline, then turn the seedling-to-green-bean coefficient into an auditable quantity in the same ledger; only then can a premium be converted from plantlets. Until then it is quoted per tonne and settled per batch.

A second basis question: who pays the premium, and where it is realised. If the buyer recognises only a batch of green beans and not the seedlings, nursery abatement is monetised through the carbon label on the downstream grow-out batch, and the allocation rule between them (by mass, by economic value, or separate accounting per stage) must be written into the contract, or the allocation dispute in §6.7 follows. Position taken here: the two stages are accounted and labelled separately, and the nursery transfers neither emissions nor rights to the grow-out stage.

06 / RISK & COMPLIANCE

Risk and compliance: twelve routes by which this design becomes a liability

claims · double counting · baseline · MRV independence · data gaps · allocation · regulation

This is a list of design constraints, not a disclaimer. Each item states an exposure and a control, and converts directly into a contract clause, a label-printing rule or an internal audit item.

01 Never call it a carbon credit

Points are not an offset; a buyer may not use them to claim neutrality, and they are not transferable, tradable or connected to any registry. A single appearance of credit, offset, neutral or net zero in copy, contract or sales talk misstates what the product is.

not tradable, not an offset, redeemable against our products only; externally they are "low-carbon points" only. Contract, label and SaaS copy must use the same wording, reviewed quarterly.

02 Double counting

Three kinds: (1) one batch declared by two growers; (2) one device's data counted into two batches; (3) nursery-stage abatement claimed again downstream as the buyer's own reduction. The third is the hardest to see.

The batch id is globally unique and bound to the device serial; one device belongs to one batch per period; and the label and contract state that the reduction has been claimed upstream and may not be claimed again downstream.

03 Greenwashing

“−52%” holds only against the baseline this method defines. “Half the emissions of conventional growing”, “eco seedling”, “zero-carbon seedling” all overstep; “carbon neutral” is impossible without an offset mechanism.

Every public number carries three qualifiers: boundary (cradle-to-gate), baseline (0.42, range 0.34–0.52) and version (AR6 / factor-table version). Missing any one, it is not approved for publication.

04 The baseline is self-declared

0.42 (0.34–0.52) comes from literature and industry interviews, not from a control plot in the same region with the same variety. It contributes most of the uncertainty in the avoided-emissions figure (±42%) and sits outside our own metering.

Build two or three control plots soon (conventional soil nursery, same variety and season, metering electricity, nutrient, substrate and transport); until those data exist, publish the baseline as a range rather than a point, and mark the label “baseline: self-declared, 0.34–0.52”.

05 MRV independence

We supply the algorithm and the ledger and would also collect the verification fee — a structural conflict. Self-verification plus self-billing is exactly what a buyer's due diligence questions, and it can disqualify the body outright.

Verification must come from a third party; the platform only does data preparation; the verifier receives a read-only replica and signed material, and does not report through the business team; and the 40% of the service fee the platform retains must be demonstrably a data-processing fee, not payment for a conclusion.

06 Data gaps and fallback estimates

Gateways drop offline, meters get replaced, batches cross month-ends, sensors drift — all of it loses intervals. Falling back to an estimate drops an A straight to a C, and must be disclosed: never filled in silently.

Missing <5%: impute the median of the same time band over the previous seven days on the same device, flag it grade B; >5% or a key line item missing: grade C, apply a 0.8 multiplier, and in the label record estimation state the method and the coverage. Above 20% missing, no label is issued.

07 Allocation between nursery and green-fruit stages

The two stages share equipment and people. Pushing all nursery emissions onto the nursery and keeping all the benefit for green fruit — or the reverse — distorts the label. The allocation rule (mass, economic value or separate accounting) must be fixed in advance and disclosed.

The rule used here: the two stages are accounted and issued separately, with no cross-stage allocation; shared equipment is split by machine hours (kwh_total attribution), while people and buildings are not allocated (declared excluded). Changing the allocation rule requires a version bump.

08 Chinese regulatory context: CCER and tanpuhui pending legal review

The national voluntary market (CCER) runs methodology → registration → verification → trading, and requires an approved methodology plus an accredited validator/verifier. Local voluntary schemes (tanpuhui) are mostly consumer-side and cannot enter the national market; they are used for redemption and incentives.

This mechanism is neither a CCER project nor a registered tanpuhui project; it is a product footprint label plus a company's own points. Public material may not say the reduction is tradable, offsettable or part of a carbon market; whether local registration is required must be confirmed with counsel and the competent authority. No public methodology exists yet for coffee nurseries or aeroponics; it cannot be assumed to be developable.

09 Drifting electricity-factor conventions

Marginal versus average, provincial versus regional grid, hourly versus annual, with or without green-tariff trades — mixing these conventions makes the same kilowatt-hour differ two- to three-fold. Whether green certificates may be deducted, and how to avoid double deduction against the grid factor, must also be settled.

The label embeds the full factor table and its version; GHG Protocol Scope 2 is disclosed both ways (location-based and market-based); where green power is purchased it is deducted with evidence retained, so we never use green power and the national average factor at once.

10 Boundary drift and multi-tenant attribution

Equipment added or removed, a greenhouse rebuilt, one gateway serving several growers, batches merged across sites — each breaks the assumption that this meter powered this batch. When the boundary moves, history stops being comparable.

Device–greenhouse–batch binding is written into a registry (reusing /api/overview entities); a change requires a change order and a batch split; cross-site merges are always split rather than issued jointly.

11 Points accounting and consumer rights

Points are a liability, not revenue. A vague redemption rate, validity period or expiry rule becomes an unfair contract term (growers as businesses need a separate set of rules).

Every points entry is logged (issue, redeem, expire, adjust); the terms state validity and the right to change; a redemption-rate change may only apply to unissued batches; the provision is based on expected redemption and re-estimated each quarter.

12 Misclaiming biogenic carbon

A seedling does absorb CO2 as it grows, but under ISO 14067 that is not part of the product footprint and may not offset emissions. Calling it sequestration or carbon negative is the most common claim, and the easiest to dismiss on sight.

The boundary states explicitly that biogenic carbon is excluded and may not be offset; any material mentioning sequestration must also say that short-lived biogenic storage is not a permanent removal and sits outside this label's accounting.
